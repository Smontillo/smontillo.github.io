I received my B.S. in Chemistry in 2020 from the Universidad de Antioquia, Colombia, under the supervision of Prof. Albeiro Restrepo. I investigated the absorption of ibuprofen into lipid bilayers by developing Python libraries that interfaced classical molecular-dynamics software (GROMACS) with electronic-structure packages (Gaussian). This QM/MM framework enabled the characterization of ibuprofen’s bonding evolution during membrane insertion and the analysis of its spectroscopic signatures.

I completed my Ph.D. in Chemistry at the University of Rochester in 2026 under the supervision of Prof. Frank (Pengfei) Huo. My doctoral research focused on understanding how strong light–matter interactions modify chemical reactivity by employing open-quantum-systems simulation frameworks, developing mixed quantum–classical (MQC) dynamics methods, and formulating rate theories that capture these reactivity changes. I also collaborated closely with experimental groups at the University of Texas at Austin to interpret and explain their observations.
